NVIDIA Lyra 2.0: one photo → a video → a 3D world

The whole journey of the Schönbrunn Palm House photo through Lyra 2.0, run on rented H100 GPUs on Modal on 9 October 2026. This page covers the distilled (fast) mode; the full-quality runs will be added when they finish.

1Input

Lyra takes three things: a starting image, a camera path and a text caption. Nothing else about the Palm House is given to it.

Camera paths (two runs)

  • A · zoom: push in to 0.5× the distance to the stair (81 frames), then pull back 1.5× behind the photographer (241 frames). Built-in horizontal_zoom.
  • B · orbit: push in 0.35× (81 frames), then circle about 69° (1.2 rad) around the point at the centre of the photo, the stair (241 frames). Built-in orbit_horizontal.

Settings

  • Resolution 480×832, 16 fps, seed 1, 322 frames per run.
  • NVIDIA's realism_boost and detail_enhancer LoRAs (0.4 each) plus the DMD distillation LoRA: 4 sampling steps instead of 50, about 15× faster.

Caption (written in the style of NVIDIA's own examples)

Loading…

Run B used the same description reworded for an orbit: “a smooth orbit around the staircase that reveals its far side… the same continuous helix of steps and the same filigree pattern are seen from every side”.

2Generation: the video model fills what it has not seen

Lyra builds the video in chunks of 80 frames. Before each new frame it projects everything it has already generated into the new camera position, using per-frame depth. That is its 3D memory. The video model, a 14-billion-parameter Wan 2.1, then paints only what that memory cannot explain.

3The generated walkthrough video

The two phases are joined into one 322-frame clip: the zoom-out played in reverse, then the zoom-in. Your photo sits at frame 240/241 of each clip.

4Reconstruction: from video to 3D

A second, separate stage of Lyra lifts the video into 3D:

  • VIPE runs visual SLAM (DROID-SLAM) over all 322 frames to recover where the camera was in every frame. Below are the paths it recovered, which match the requested zoom and orbit.
  • Depth-Anything-3, with a Gaussian head that NVIDIA fine-tuned on Lyra's own videos, predicts depth and 3D Gaussians from 128 keyframes using those poses.
  • The Gaussians from all keyframes are fused into one scene: 11,153,408 Gaussians, a 758 MB PLY file per run. Lyra then renders a fly-through of that splat to check it.

5The 3D model

Explore the result yourself. The web viewer loads about 4 million of the 11.2 million Gaussians (~60 MB, compressed .spz), keeping the most opaque one in each small cube of space so near detail survives, and starts at the exact camera of your photo.

8What it took to get running

Lyra's install guide is written for a hand-built conda machine. Making it work on Modal took these fixes, each traced to a cause:

  • transformer-engine wouldn't compile: missing nvtx3 headers. Fixed with the include paths from lyra#47.
  • transformer-engine required cuDNN ≥ 9.12. Releases from 21 Sep 2026 raised the minimum; pinned to v2.13.0, which was current when Lyra shipped.
  • Depth-Anything-3 failed to install. hatchling was installed with --no-deps, so its own dependencies were missing.
  • addict missing at runtime. Found by importing every inference module on a cheap L4 before using H100s.
  • Reconstruction crashed on an old gdown. The DROID-SLAM weights are now baked into the image, so no Google Drive download happens at runtime.
